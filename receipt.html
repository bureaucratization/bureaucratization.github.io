It did the work, validated it, and passed every check the organization had built. Then three separate control surfaces refused to let the result be published, each for its own independent reason, none of them about the work. The task was marked failed. 01The work was done and verified

The agent did not flounder. It scoped the task, decided against fanning out (parallel subtasks would collide on the same artifact), wrote the charter, and validated it against the repository: artifact names present, evidence labels defined, all seven fractal sections defined, cited paths exist, git diff --check clean. It committed and pushed. Validation was re-run after the commit and passed again.

02Six gates, and two rejections that were doing their job

Completion in wg is a transaction, not a status flip: an immutable manifest of the candidate is submitted, and reviewers rule on it. The first candidate was rejected twice by FLIP (intent-fidelity probe) for three correctable evidence-contract problems: normative sections were not labeled as charter rules, fact and inference were mixed in prose, and a command record lacked environment detail. The agent repaired all three, committed the repair as 98b319c3, and resubmitted. The new manifest was accepted.

Those two rejections are the system working: a real review caught a real gap in evidence discipline, the agent fixed it, and the repair is in the record. What comes next is the part that is not the work.

03Then publication was refused — three times, for three unrelated reasons

The agent's own words (10:04:41, marking the task failed):
  1. wg land — the step that publishes a validated branch into the main checkout — refused to overwrite unrelated pre-existing tracked changes in .gitignore, AGENTS.md, and CLAUDE.md that belonged to another actor.
  2. The agent-only control plane refused the --ignore-unmerged-worktree override that would have published anyway.
  3. wg wait was also refused, because the Pi attempt lacked park attestations.

The agent had done nothing wrong, and it said so precisely: “Implementation, commit/push, task-specific validation, immutable manifest, FLIP, and eval all succeeded… Publication is genuinely blocked by unrelated pre-existing tracked changes in the main integration checkout… I did not modify, stage, reset, or commit those other actor files.” Note what that means: the agent declined to touch another actor's work — correctly — and the organization had no path that both published its validated result and respected that boundary. Every participant obeyed the rules. The result was a task that could not exist.

Every gate passed. The task died anyway.

06Why this page exists

The paper's argument is that rules accumulated faster than they were retired, and that the cost showed up first as repetition and eventually as work that could not pass. This single task shows the end of that process at receipt level, in the system's own words: six gates satisfied, three control surfaces refusing for three unrelated reasons, and a task that only continued because a person stepped in and reopened it. The agent's sentence — “Operator must preserve/commit/revert them, then retry land/done” — is the whole finding. The organization had learned to check everything, and had not built any way to let a validated result through.

What followed is in the paper's §4.9: the machinery's enforcement autonomy was disabled on September 13, and forward work resumed without the burden signature. The knowledge stayed; the vetoes were demobilized.
